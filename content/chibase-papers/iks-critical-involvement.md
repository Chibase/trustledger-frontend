Indigenous Knowledge Systems are not a courtesy paragraph in an ESIA. In South African development practice they are a method for participation, monitoring, and legitimacy — or they are theatre.

A 2026 practitioner working paper (Zenodo) argues for a move away from compliance-based consultation toward operational systems of community-led engagement. The claim is practical: projects last when communities are critically involved, not merely notified.

What that looks like in the field:

- Named counterparts, including traditional authorities, sit in the register — not “the community” as a blur.
- Place is specific: ward, municipality, customary geography. A floating spreadsheet is not participation.
- Engagements are recorded. Commitments have owners and dates. Grievances have a case path.
- MEL cites the trail (who was in the room, what was promised, what closed) instead of reconstructing a month-end pack from chat exports.

Chibase Consulting treats IKS as mainstream method for facilitation and M&E. The complementary SRM desk for operators who need that trail to survive oversight is TrustLedger — a separate product, not a co-branded hero.
